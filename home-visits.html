Who it suits

A home visit costs a bit more than coming to St Anne's House, and for plenty of people it is worth every penny of the difference.

If getting out is the hard part

Older clients, anyone recovering from illness or surgery, anyone whose mobility makes an unfamiliar building a genuine obstacle. This is who home visits were made for.

If there's a baby asleep upstairs

New parents rarely get an hour anywhere. If someone can watch the baby in the next room, you can have one without leaving the house.

If you just want to stay put

The best part of a massage is the twenty minutes afterwards. At home you get to keep them, rather than spending them finding your keys.

A note on why home visits are for women

I work alone, and a home visit means going into a stranger's house by myself. Most therapists I know who do mobile work have a story about a booking that turned out not to be a booking at all, and quite a few have stopped doing home visits altogether because of it.

I would rather keep offering them. So I offer them to women.

It is not a judgement about anyone in particular, and it isn't about the men who come to see me at St Anne's House — treatments there are open to everybody, and always will be. It is simply the arrangement that lets me carry on doing the part of this work that reaches the people who need it most.

If you are a man looking for massage at home, I am sorry to turn you away, and I would rather be honest about it than quietly not reply. Come and see me in Brislington instead — you will get exactly the same treatment.

What I need from you

Not much, and nothing you need to buy. But it's worth knowing before you book.

  • A space roughly 3m by 2m — enough for the couch with room for me to walk around it. A cleared living room floor is usually perfect.
  • A warm room. Warmer than you think. Put the heating on twenty minutes before I arrive.
  • Somewhere to park, or a note on where to find a space. I am carrying a folded massage couch, so a long walk from the car eats into your treatment time.
  • A quiet hour. Dogs shut in another room if they are the enthusiastic kind, and phones on silent.
  • Somewhere to wash my hands before and after. That's it.

Booking a home visit

Slightly more involved than booking at the clinic, and here's exactly why.

  1. Get in touch with your full name and address. I need both before I can confirm — I let someone know where I am working and when, every time.
  2. A short conversation first. Usually a few messages. I'll ask what you're after, whether you've had massage before, and about anything medical I should know.
  3. A £20 deposit secures the slot. It comes off the treatment price. I ask for this on home visits because I'm turning down clinic bookings and travelling across the city to be there.
  4. I arrive, set up, and we begin. Allow fifteen minutes either side of the treatment itself for setting up and packing down.

And one thing that shouldn't need saying

This is professional therapeutic massage. Any suggestion otherwise — in a message, in an enquiry, or during a treatment — means the session ends immediately, the full fee is still payable, and I don't work with you again.

I know that anyone actually reading this page is not the person that paragraph is aimed at. Thank you for bearing with it.
