About

Hello, I'm Fleur.

I teach yoga and I do massage, in Bristol and Bath. The two are more closely related than they look.

How I got here

I have been teaching yoga for over five years. I trained in massage because I kept meeting the same thing in class: people carrying tension they could not breathe or stretch their way out of. You can tell someone to soften their shoulders for an hour and watch them not be able to. Some things need hands.

Teaching still shapes how I work. Five years of watching bodies move — of noticing which hip does not rotate and which shoulder sits higher — turns out to be a decent preparation for finding what is actually going on under someone's skin.

How I work

Slowly. I would rather spend twenty minutes on one shoulder that needs it than run through a routine from neck to feet because that is the shape of an hour.

I will ask questions at the start, and check in on pressure as we go. After that you do not have to talk. Plenty of people arrive wanting to chat and go quiet ten minutes in, and that is exactly as it should be — you are not there to entertain me.

I will also tell you honestly what I think. If I think you need a physio rather than a massage, I will say so. If I think you would get more from coming every three weeks for a while than from booking one long treatment now, I will say that too.

Where you'll find me

Most of my clinic work is at St Anne's House in Brislington — a community building in south Bristol run by a local charity, with a warm room, easy parking and none of the strip-lit clinical feel that puts people off. I also treat in Bath most weeks.

And I do home visits across both cities, which for older clients and new parents is often the only version of this that actually works.

The name

Glisk is a Scots word. It means a gleam — a brief flash of light, sunlight caught through a break in the clouds, a short warmth from a fire that has burned low.

It seemed like the right word for what an hour on the table can give you.
