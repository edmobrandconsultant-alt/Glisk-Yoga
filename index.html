How it works

Hello, I'm Fleur

I have been teaching yoga for over five years, and I trained in massage because I kept meeting the same thing in class: people carrying tension they could not breathe or stretch their way out of. Some things need hands.

My work is slow. I would rather spend twenty minutes on one shoulder that needs it than run through a routine. I will ask questions at the start, check in on pressure as we go, and then let you be quiet — you do not have to make conversation.

Questions

And if yours isn't here, just ask — no obligation.

Will deep tissue hurt?

It shouldn't. Deep tissue means slow and specific, not brutal. There can be a strong sensation when we find something that has been holding for a while, but it should stay in the range where you can breathe and stay relaxed. I check in as we go and you can ask me to ease off at any point, always.

Which treatment should I book?

If you have a specific area that aches or you train hard, book deep tissue. If you are stressed, not sleeping, or just need to stop, book relaxation. If you are unsure, book either and tell me on the day — I tailor it once we've talked, and there is no price difference.

60 or 90 minutes?

Ninety, if you can. Sixty is a good treatment but it means choosing where to focus. Ninety means we cover the back, hips and neck properly, and it gives you long enough to genuinely switch off rather than just starting to.

Do you do home visits?

Yes, in Bristol and Bath — and for women only. That is a safety decision rather than a preference, and I have explained the reasoning honestly on the home visits page. Treatments at St Anne's House are open to everyone.

What do I need to do?

Nothing. Arrive a few minutes early, wear something comfortable. You'll undress to your underwear and be covered by towels throughout, with only the area being worked on uncovered. Drink some water afterwards and don't book anything strenuous straight after if you can help it.

What's your cancellation policy?

Please give 24 hours' notice if you need to move or cancel, so the slot can go to someone else. Life happens, and I'm reasonable about it.
